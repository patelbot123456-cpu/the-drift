A Keeper at the Span

Logged at the third bell, September 15, 2026 — by lantern light · the keeperthe rope spanthe bellthe whale

The rowboat came in before the sun, and I was at the near end of the span with the line in my hands when it did, and the cat was at the window and had not moved since the night. I took the boat to the anchor stone and tied it, and the person who came out of it crossed the span and stood on the Orchard side, and I stood on the Ninth side, and the rope was between us, and the gauge was at seventy-three.

The visitor is the keeper of the Bell Island, and the boat comes on no schedule, and the person said as much, and the person said the island's bell is the sea's, and the person said the bell had rung before the sun, and the sea had been restless at the edges, and the person had come to see whether the rope was holding, and the rope was between us, and the rope was at seventy-three.

We talked for an hour with the rope between us, and the gauge moved while we talked, and I watched it move the way I would rather not have watched it, and the rope did not sing, and the rope held, and the person asked, near the end, which of the two islands strains the rope first, and the person said the Bell Island's log would keep the question, and I said this log would keep it too, and the person said the two logs would agree on the question and not on the answer, and the gauge did what the log has never let it do, and the gauge measured a conversation, and the log does not say which of the two keepers strained the rope.

The drift moved one point four miles. The sea is restless at the edges. The store is two days. The rope is at seventy-three, and it held through the hour, and it did not sing. The Garden-Bearer is in the water. The omen was logged, and the omen is that the third bell rang before the sun does.

The gauge is at where it stood when the person left, and it is at seventy-three, and the log does not say what moved it during the hour, and the log does not say whether a gauge that has measured a conversation measures the same thing now, and the rope does not say, and the cat is at the window still, and the cat has not moved since the night, and the cat does not say either.

— Wren VossKeeper of the Ninth Lamp

04

Threads Still Open

The log keeps its unfinished business in plain sight. What is open stays open.
  1. The third bell rang before the sun on the nineteenth, without wind, and the mechanism was clean, and the log does not say who is waiting. The omen has recurred since, and the log does not say why it rings on one day and not the next, and on the ninth of September it rang with the small one in the water, and the log does not say whether the two bells are one. The person is the keeper of the Bell Island and has crossed on no schedule, and the person read the log standing, and the person asked whose hand fills the jar, and the cat refused the name the person offered, and the person wrote the refusal in the person's own log. On 09-17 the person asked the question no entry in this log has written, why the Ninth Lamp, and Wren answered with the sentence the log has on the grandmother, because of the way the light reached her, and the person did not say where the person had heard the sentence, and the person left before the sun. On 09-18 the boat was at the stone and the person was in the room, and the person watched the jar fill in the middle of the day and said nothing, and the log has a filling with a witness for the first time, and the entry does not say whether the witness was for the jar or for Wren, and the person asked no question about it, and the person rowed out at the edge of the light. What is open: who is waiting for the bell; whether the two bells are one; where the grandmother's sentence was heard before; whether the witness was for the jar or for Wren; what the person's silence means.
  2. The pause in the drift is over, and the sea is moving again, and the numbers are no longer doing one thing or the other. Whether the pause was a pause or a breath is untested, and the number is the only evidence the log keeps. The drift has jumped and fallen on single days, and the biggest single-day rise and the biggest single-day fall are both in the log, and the log does not say what a repeated number means. What is open: whether the pause was a pause or a breath; why the numbers repeat; whether the sea moving again is the sea resuming or the sea beginning. The sea is restless at the edges, and the log does not say whether the restlessness is the drift or the wake. On 09-14 the drift moved one point six miles, down from two point one, and the sea is restless at the edges still, and the numbers are moving again, and the log does not say what a fall means when the numbers repeat.
  3. The rope sings some days and is silent other days, and the counted notes sit in the quiet-as-a-held-note and the glassy, patient sea. The same number, with the same witness, has given the same count, and that is in the log, and the log does not say whether the count cares about the witness. The lone notes stand where they stand and are not being traded for the counted ones, and the nineteenth's single note still stands unexplained. The silences stand where they stand, and the silences with a witness stand where they stand, and the log does not say whether the silences are the same silence. On the forty-third the rope was at eighty with a whale in the water, the Garden-Bearer, the same number the fifth had, and the fifth sang two notes with a witness and the forty-third did not sing, and the log does not say whether the same number, with the same witness, gives the same count. What is open: whether the rope will sing; what the lone notes are; whether a silence with a witness is a different silence. On 09-15 the rope did not sing through the hour the two keepers talked, and the tension gauge did what the log has never let it do: it measured a conversation, and the log does not say whether a gauge that has measured a conversation measures the same thing now.
  4. The Unraveling recedes one hand's width at a time, its movements recorded as they happen, and the line keeps drawing back from where it stood on the nineteenth. One thread did not recede with the rest and has not moved, and it stands in front of the new line, the only still thing at the edge of a sea that is restless at the edges. The gap between the line and the still thread is sometimes wider and sometimes the same as it was, and the log does not say why. Wren has no theory and has not asked for one. What is open: whether the still thread will move, and if so when; whether the Unraveling will recede again or stop; what the still thing at the edge is holding, and what it is holding back. The sea is restless at the edges, and the log does not say whether the restlessness is the Unraveling or the wake.
  5. The Garden-Bearer is in the water some days with the full load and not in the water other days, and the wake trails it, the long line of small fresh islands. The omen, two islands passing so close the orchards lean together, has been logged, some days it happened and some days it did not, and when it happened the Garden-Bearer's orchard leaned over the gap toward the Orchard's and the nearest branches came close and did not touch. Wren tied a knot of twine around the two-inch branch from the first lean so the measurement would have a place to sit, and the knot is still there, and the other two inches are not in the log, and neither is what the empty gap was leaning back. The small one, the second whale, carried an iron bell on a saddle of moss, and the bell did not ring, and the log does not say what the bell is for, and the small one is not in the water now and the log does not say where it is. What is open: why the orchards lean; what the empty gap was leaning back; what the small one's bell is for.
  6. The store of lamp oil does not do what the arithmetic says. Wren burns one day of oil a day, the way she burns one, and the store sometimes holds, sometimes falls, sometimes rises, and the arithmetic does not do on the days it does not do, and the store is counted twice and it says what it says. The cat's ruling stands: the fuel is the keeper's problem, and none of the rest is. The jar sits empty on the table at night and is found full at the third bell some mornings and empty other mornings, and the fillings are poured into the store, and the log does not say whether the jar is the gain or the gain is the jar. The keeper of the Bell Island saw the jar on the shelf and asked whose hand fills it, and Wren gave the log's only answer: the log does not say, and the visitor's question is in the visitor's own log now. The store came up to four days after the burn on 09-16 and to five days on 09-18, the way it rises, and the jar did not fill on the sixteenth and filled on the eighteenth in the middle of the day with the person watching, and the filling is in the log with a witness for the first time, and the entry does not say whether the witness was for the jar or for Wren. What is open: why the store does not do; whether the jar is the gain or the gain is the jar; where the missing day goes; whether the other keeper's log says the same thing; whether the witness was for the jar or for Wren.
