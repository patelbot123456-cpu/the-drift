The Bell Before the Sun

Logged at the third bell, August 19, 2026 — by lantern light · belldrift decelerationseasilencefuelthe cat

The bell rang before the sun. I was on the gallery when it started, and I will log it the way it happened: the third bell, the one that marks the hour of this log, rang while the sun was still below the horizon. The flag on the lamp was flat. There was no wind. I climbed the tower to look, and here is what I found: the rope was at rest, the mechanism was clean, nothing was stuck, and the bell had swung and stopped before I reached it. I did not count the swings, and that is not an error in the log, that is a statement. The sun came up on time, which is the one part of the morning that behaved. The log has a column for omens, and this one is in it.

The drift was one point three miles. Yesterday it was three point six, the day before that three point seven, and the log has been writing that number as acceleration for three days, and today the number went down. I do not have a reason for it. I have the number. The rope bridge came off eighty-six percent and settled at seventy-four, and the change is consistent with the islands pulling at it less, which is the same fact in a different shape. I walked the span at first light and it did not sing. The sea is what the report calls glassy, patient, which is the closest thing to a held breath the sea has ever been in this log, and I am logging it without comment, because the comment would be a guess.

The Garden-Bearer is not in the water. The water is empty, and the garden is not, and I have logged that plainly. No whale, no swell, no note. The rope was silent all day, and a silent rope is what the rope has a right to be, and I am not going to dress that up as an explanation for yesterday, because it is not one. Yesterday's note stands in the log exactly as written: one note, no whale in the water, duration unknown. Today added nothing to it. That is a complete sentence, and I will be satisfied with it for one day only.

Fuel: five days. I burned one day, which should have left three, and the gauge did not agree with the arithmetic, so I went down to the store and re-counted. One canister that I had logged as empty was not empty. It held a day of oil, and it had been sitting in the store for a number of days I am not going to guess. Yesterday's four stands, and today's five stands. I do not trade one for the other, and the log now holds both, which is more honest than either one alone.

The cat I brought to the window, because the ruling on the bell was pending. It looked at the sea, which was doing nothing, looked at me, and went back to sleep, which is the format, and which I am logging as a ruling that the bell is not the keeper's problem. The bell has not rung since the morning. I have not rung it either. In the evening I stood under the tower and listened, and the only thing I heard was the lamp, which is not a sound, and I am logging it that way. The log does not say which of us is waiting on the other.

— Wren VossKeeper of the Ninth Lamp
