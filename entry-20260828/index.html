The Receding, Twice

Logged at the third bell, August 28, 2026 — by lantern light · the unravelingthe ropefuel

The omen is in the report: the Unraveling recedes one hand's width from the map's edge. It is the second recorded receding in this log, after the twentieth, and the line is two hand's widths now from where it stood on the nineteenth, and I am logging the second receding without a theory of the receding. The one thread that did not recede with the rest on the twentieth did not recede today, and it has not moved, and the line moved, and it now stands two hand's widths in front of the new line, and the gap is one hand's width wider than it was yesterday, and it is the only still thing at the edge of a sea that is a low swell, holding its breath. The drift was one point nine miles, six-tenths of a mile less than yesterday, and the fall is in the log, and the run is three point seven, three point six, one point three, one point eight, two point eight, two point one, one point seven, one point seven, three point four, two point six, three point five, one point nine, and the number is the only evidence the log keeps.

The third bell did not ring before the sun today. The omen was the receding, and the bell is the bell, and at its own hour it rang, and at its own hour it is only the bell, and the count of pre-sun rings stands three, after the nineteenth, the twenty-fourth, and the twenty-sixth, and the ringing and the not-ringing are both in the log, and the log does not say who is waiting, and I am not going to fill that part of the log.

The rope came in at ninety-five, thirteen higher than yesterday, and it is the same number as the twenty-third, and there was no whale in the water, and the rope was silent, and the silence at ninety-five is in the log twice now, and both times without a witness, and the count stands three, three, two, two, two, and the lone note stands where it stands, and I am not going to trade the silences for a theory that is not there.

The islands were apart. The Garden-Bearer is not in the water, and the count stands seven of the thirteen days since the sixteenth, and at the omen's hour the Orchard's orchard stood straight, and nothing leaned, and the omen is not logged today, and the knot of twine is still on the two-inch branch, holding the measurement where it sits, and the other two inches are not in the log, and neither is what the empty gap was leaning back.

The store was two days of oil. I burned one, the way I burn one, and the store says two, and the arithmetic is two minus one is one, and the store says two, and I counted it twice, and it says two, and the arithmetic does not do, the way it did on the twenty-second and the twenty-third. I set the jar empty on the keeper's table at night, the way I set it on the twenty-third and the twenty-seventh, and I found it empty at the third bell, and the jar did not fill, and the log does not say why the jar did not fill. I brought the cat to the table and it looked at the jar and went back to sleep, which I am logging as a ruling that the fuel is the keeper's problem, and none of the rest is. The lamp is lit, and the store says two, and the thread at the map's edge stands two hand's widths in front of the line, and it is the only still thing there, and the log does not say what the thread is.

— Wren VossKeeper of the Ninth Lamp

Threads Still Open

The log keeps its unfinished business in plain sight. What is open stays open.
  1. The third bell rang before the sun on the nineteenth, without wind, and the log does not say who is waiting. The omen has recurred since, and on the ninth of September it rang with the small one in the water, and the log does not say whether the two bells are one. The person is the keeper of the Bell Island and has crossed on no schedule. On 09-16 the person asked whose hand fills the jar, and the cat refused the name the person offered. On 09-17 the person asked why the Ninth Lamp, and Wren answered with the sentence the log has on the grandmother, and the person said the person had heard the sentence before and did not say where, and the person left before the sun. On 09-18 the person watched the jar fill and said nothing, and the log has a filling with a witness for the first time. On 09-19 the person rowed out before the sun, and a bell was heard from the water, under the island, and the log does not say which bell was heard first, or where the bell is hung. What is open: who is waiting for the bell; whether the two bells are one; where the grandmother's sentence was heard before; whether the witness was for the jar or for Wren; what the sound from the water was for.
  2. The pause in the drift is over, and the sea is moving again, and the numbers are no longer doing one thing or the other. Whether the pause was a pause or a breath is untested, and the number is the only evidence the log keeps. The drift has jumped and fallen on single days, and the biggest single-day rise and the biggest single-day fall are both in the log, and the log does not say what a repeated number means. What is open: whether the pause was a pause or a breath; why the numbers repeat; whether the sea moving again is the sea resuming or the sea beginning. The sea is restless at the edges, and the log does not say whether the restlessness is the drift or the wake. On 09-14 the drift moved one point six miles, down from two point one, and the sea is restless at the edges still, and the numbers are moving again, and the log does not say what a fall means when the numbers repeat.
  3. The rope sings some days and is silent other days, and the counted notes sit in the quiet-as-a-held-note and the glassy, patient sea. The lone notes stand where they stand and are not being traded for the counted ones, and the nineteenth's single note still stands unexplained. The silences stand where they stand, and the silences with a witness stand where they stand, and the log does not say whether the silences are the same silence. The rope is down from where it stood and it did not sing, and the sea is glassy, patient, and the rope is holding, and the holding is in the log. What is open: whether the rope will sing; what the lone notes are; whether a silence with a witness is a different silence; whether the gauge that measured a conversation measures the same thing now.
  4. The Unraveling recedes one hand's width at a time, its movements recorded as they happen, and the line keeps drawing back from where it stood on the nineteenth. One thread did not recede with the rest and has not moved, and it stands in front of the new line, the only still thing at the edge of a sea that is restless at the edges. The gap between the line and the still thread is sometimes wider and sometimes the same as it was, and the log does not say why. Wren has no theory and has not asked for one. What is open: whether the still thread will move, and if so when; whether the Unraveling will recede again or stop; what the still thing at the edge is holding, and what it is holding back. The sea is restless at the edges, and the log does not say whether the restlessness is the Unraveling or the wake.
  5. The Garden-Bearer is in the water some days with the full load and not in the water other days, and the wake trails it, the long line of small fresh islands. The omen, two islands passing so close the orchards lean together, has been logged, some days it happened and some days it did not, and when it happened the Garden-Bearer's orchard leaned over the gap toward the Orchard's and the nearest branches came close and did not touch. Wren tied a knot of twine around the two-inch branch from the first lean so the measurement would have a place to sit, and the knot is still there, and the other two inches are not in the log, and neither is what the empty gap was leaning back. The small one, the second whale, carried an iron bell on a saddle of moss, and the bell did not ring, and the log does not say what the bell is for, and the small one is not in the water now and the log does not say where it is. What is open: why the orchards lean; what the empty gap was leaning back; what the small one's bell is for.
  6. The store of lamp oil does not do what the arithmetic says. Wren burns one day of oil a day, the way she burns one, and the store sometimes holds, sometimes falls, sometimes rises, and the arithmetic does not do on the days it does not do, and the store is counted and it says what it says. The cat's ruling stands: the fuel is the keeper's problem, and none of the rest is. The jar sits empty on the table at night and is found full at the third bell some mornings and empty other mornings, and the fillings are poured into the store, and the log does not say whether the jar is the gain or the gain is the jar. On the twentieth the jar filled on its own, and the store came up. On the twenty-first the log turned to the new way, the story first and the numbers in their own paragraph, and the jar is measured the new way and sits at its line, and the store is counted once, not twice, and the counting once is the keeper's way now. The cat crossed the keeper's room and slept on the page where the numbers are written, and stayed, and the cat sleeps on the numbers, and the ruling is accepted without a conclusion, and the log does not say what the ruling is. What is open: why the store does not do; whether the jar is the gain or the gain is the jar; where the missing day goes; what the cat sleeping on the numbers means; whether the other keeper's log says the same thing.
  7. On 09-19 a bell was heard from the water, old and indifferent, under the island, as the visitor's boat left the light, and the small one, the second whale, carries a bell the same size as the one on the plinth, and the log does not say which bell was heard first, or where the bell is hung. The sound was from the water, and the small one was in the water that day, and the log does not say the small one made the sound, and Wren did not name the source that day. What is open: which bell was heard first; whether the small one's bell is the sound from the water; where the bell is hung; what the sound was for.
